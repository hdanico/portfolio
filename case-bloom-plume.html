Bloom & Plume Coffee

Bloom & Plume the florist makes $200 arrangements. Bloom & Plume Coffee had to put that same world in a $5 cup — in Historic Filipinotown, in a room built for everyone.

Role
Art Direction, Brand Illustration, Hand Lettering, Packaging & Merch, Web
Creative Director
Maurice Harris
Timeline
2018 – 2019
Client
Bloom & Plume Coffee
Press
The Infatuation — “Magic and Resistance”

01 — The Brief

A $200 florist, in a $5 cup.

Maurice Harris built Bloom & Plume as floral art — lush, Black, unapologetically ornamental, and mostly seen at $200 a stem. With his brother Moses he wanted a second front door: a coffee shop in Historic Filipinotown, next to the flower studio, that anyone could walk into.

Under Maurice's direction, my job was to carry the Bloom & Plume feeling onto the cheapest thing in the room — a paper cup — and everything around it: the illustration, the packaging, the shop, the website, the merch.

02 — Discovery

Beauty and power, which Maurice never separates.

His arrangements are one half of the reference — the color, the density, the refusal of a safe neutral. The other half is what the café is for: in his words, a space “filled with color, from a person of color's perspective, that centers people of color.” The identity had to hold both.

  • — Palette pulled straight from Maurice's florals
  • — African heritage referenced directly, not as decoration
  • — Every surface speced to print cheap and reorder often
03 — Design System

The Bloom & Plume seal already existed. Everything drawn on top of it was new: an icon, a botanical illustration system, hand lettering for every headline, and the objects it all lands on.

The fist and the rose

I drew the café's icon — a raised fist gripping a single rose, beauty and power in one line. It runs as neon on the wall and across every piece of merch.

A drawn system, hand-lettered

Loose botanical drawings pulled from Maurice's arrangements, and hand lettering on every headline — including the café's tagline, Thirst Come, Thirst Served — all of it mine, across the cups, bags, fans and merch.

A Kente cloth of the silhouette

The family's African heritage, referenced directly: a Kente-cloth pattern built from repeats of the Bloom & Plume head-in-keyhole silhouette. I designed and sourced the cups it prints on.

“Oh Taste and See”

Church-style hand fans, designed and sourced — a Black-Southern-church object dropped into a coffee shop, doing exactly the work the whole brand does.

04 — Results

Bloom & Plume Coffee opened in Historic Filipinotown in 2019 and still runs on the same drawings — the fist and the rose, the florals, the Kente silhouette. Thirst come, thirst served, in my hand lettering, on the cup and on the shirt.
